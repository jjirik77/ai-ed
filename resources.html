    Kit item 2 · Module 1

    AI, automation, or neither? Card sort and misconception cards

    Cut apart. Teams sort each scenario, then defend one placement to another team. Digital version in Module 1.

    ScenarioAIAutomationNeither
    The LMS emails students when an assignment is due tomorrow.
    A chatbot drafts three versions of a lab safety quiz.
    A spreadsheet formula averages quiz scores.
    A captioning service converts lecture audio to text.
    A tutoring tool chooses the next practice problem from a student's errors.
    An instructor rearranges seats after a midterm.
    An image generator makes a diagram of the water cycle.
    A plagiarism checker matches text against a database of submissions.

    Misconception cards

    • "It looks things up."
    • "If it cites a source, the source is real."
    • "It understands what it writes."
    • "The same prompt always gives the same answer."
    • "Longer prompts are always better."
    • "An AI detector can prove a student cheated."
    • "If it is in my LMS, it is approved for any data."
    • "AI output belongs to whoever prompted it."

    Kit item 5 · Module 3

    Prompt Specification Canvas

    ElementYour specification
    Goal: what the output is for
    Context: course, level, learners
    Source material: what the model should use
    Constraints: length, reading level, what to avoid
    Desired process: steps the model should take
    Output format: structure, headings, table
    Quality criteria: how you will judge it
    Validation instructions: what you will check, and what the model should flag

    Iteration log: for each run, record what went wrong, your diagnosis, and the change. Do not reword without a diagnosis.

    Kit item 6 · Module 4

    TRACE validation checklist and red-team packet

    StepCheck✓
    T Task and stakesUse named; stakes set as low, moderate, or high
    R Review claimsEvery claim, number, quotation, and citation numbered
    A Authoritative evidenceSources opened; passages confirmed; numbers recomputed; lateral reading for surprising claims
    C Check bias, privacy, completenessMissing perspectives; stereotypes; protected data in or out
    E ExplainUse, revise, or reject recorded with a reason and open questions

    Red-team packet: print the 1918 influenza mini-lesson from the Module 4 interactive. It contains one fabricated citation, one numerical error, one missing perspective, and one overconfident claim. Facilitator answer key: lines 3, 5, 6, and 7.

    Kit item 9 · Module 7

    Assignment stress test and AI-use-level worksheet

    QuestionAnswer
    Which outcome does this assignment measure?
    Could a student complete it with AI and no learning? How?
    Which AIAS level fits the outcome? (1 No AI, 2 AI-Assisted Planning, 3 AI-Assisted Task Completion, 4 Full AI, 5 AI Exploration)
    Which redesign moves make process visible? (staged work, annotated drafts, local context, oral explanation, in-class checkpoint, process notes, source verification, AI interaction record, reflective defense)
    Does the redesign still measure the outcome, or only make AI inconvenient?
    What does the redesign cost students and you in time?
    Is it equitable for students without paid tools?

    Kit item 10 · Module 7

    Course policy and task-level disclosure templates

    Course AI policy template

    In this course, AI tools may be used [where] and may not be used [where]. The reason is [connect to course outcomes]. When you use AI, disclose it in this form: [tool category, what you asked, what you kept, what you changed]. You are responsible for the accuracy of everything you submit. Do not enter other people's personal information or your classmates' work into AI tools. If you are unsure whether a use is allowed, ask before you submit. Questions about suspected misuse will start with a conversation about your process, not a detector score. Alternative: every AI-based activity has a no-AI option. [How to request it.]

    Task-level disclosure statement

    AI use on this task: Level [1-5] ([level name]). I used AI to: ____ I kept: ____ · I changed: ____ · I checked: ____ I did not use AI for: ____

    Kit item 11 · Module 8

    Ethics case cards and decision record

    • AI-generated feedback is consistently harsher on a multilingual student's grammar than on the ideas.
    • A colleague pasted a class roster with grades into a public chatbot to draft emails.
    • An assignment works best with a paid AI tool that some students cannot afford.
    • An AI-produced handout has images without alt text and a low-contrast color scheme.
    • A department chair asks faculty to auto-grade final essays with AI to save time.
    Decision record
    Situation
    Stakeholders affected
    Principles in tension (beneficence, justice, autonomy, transparency, accountability, privacy, nondiscrimination, risk-benefit)
    Options considered
    Decision and rationale
    What would change the decision
