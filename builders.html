    Fieldwork · Weeks 1–8

    Course Build Studio

    The deliverableYour course materials go into a repository, and four prompt builders turn them into a unit framework, a lesson for each lesson in it, and activities. You review and revise every output before it reaches students.

    Builders: 4Works with: any approved AI tool that accepts filesSaves: this browser only

    How the builders work

    You fill in the form; the builder writes the prompt

    You do not need to be a prompt engineer. Each builder asks plain questions about your course, then assembles a complete prompt you can read, edit, and copy into your approved AI tool. The prompts follow the specification method from Module 3: goal, context, sources, constraints, process, format, quality criteria, and validation.

    WeekBuilderWhat you produce
    1Course profileThe unit you will build and the files you will use
    21 RepositoryA project space with your files, standing instructions, and a grounding test
    32 Unit frameworkThe unit structure: lessons, outcomes, sources, and checks
    4Check prompts + TRACEA validated framework (see Module 4)
    53 LessonsOne lesson for each lesson in the framework
    64 ActivitiesA student activity for each lesson that needs one
    7–8Modules 7 and 8The unit assessment, AI-use directions, and a 30-day pilot of the unit

    The support fades on purpose. Builders 1 and 2 write the whole prompt for you. Builder 3 leaves the quality criteria for you to write. Builder 4 leaves the parts that protect student learning for you to write. By Week 6 you are writing the parts that matter most.

    Responsible use

    Use only your institution's approved AI tool, and upload only files you are allowed to use that way. Never upload student work, names, grades, accommodations, or confidential records. Every builder output is a draft: check it with TRACE and revise it substantially before students see it.

    Local policy · fill in for your institution

    Approved tool with a project or file-upload space: [tool name and how to access it]. Library contact for licensed materials: [name, email].

    After each builder

    From draft to deliverable

    1. Run the prompt in your approved tool, inside the project that holds your files.
    2. Run the check prompt in the same conversation. It asks the tool to list unsupported claims and gaps; it does not replace your review.
    3. Apply TRACE to anything students will rely on: open the cited files, recompute numbers, and look for missing perspectives.
    4. Revise substantially. Your expertise, examples, and voice are what make the lesson yours, and they matter for authorship (see Module 5).
    5. Record it in your portfolio: where the final version is saved and what you changed.
